\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Purpose}
This document holds the use-case model of OmniCourse and the specification of each use case in it. The model shows who
uses the system and for what. Each specification says, step by step, what the actor does and how the system answers, what
must be true before the use case starts, what is true after it ends, and what happens when something goes differently.
The team builds, tests and demonstrates the product from these specifications, and the UI prototypes (PA2 item e)
follow their steps.

\subsection{Scope}
The model covers the twelve feature groups of the Vision document v2.0 (F1 to F12) for the first release: the Advanced
Program in Computer Science (APCS) at HCMUS. It has 19 use cases in five modules:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A. Account and access} (F1): sign up, sign in, and the student's control over their own account.
  \item \textbf{B. Academic record} (F2 to F4): transcript import, degree audit, grades and the curriculum explorer.
  \item \textbf{C. Planning} (F5, F6, F7, F9): the plan to graduation, the plan generator, what-if scenarios and sharing.
  \item \textbf{D. Help and reminders} (F8, F10, F11): the advisor chat, notifications and the school-task guides.
  \item \textbf{E. Curriculum administration} (F12): the curriculum, the shared course catalog and administration rights.
\end{itemize}

Out of scope: anything that acts on a school portal for the student (registration, payment), reading a portal password,
and importing a curriculum PDF with a language model. The last one was a use case in an early draft and was dropped
under ADR 0003; curriculum data is entered by people (UC-11).

\subsection{Definitions}
{\small
\begin{xltabular}{\dimexpr\textwidth-2\tabcolsep\relax}{@{}p{3.6cm}Y@{}}
\hcell{Term} & \hcell{Meaning in this document}\\\hdrline
\endhead
Program, cohort & A program of study (for example APCS) and the students who entered it in the same year. Each cohort
has its own curriculum.\\
Requirement group & A part of a curriculum with a credit minimum, such as core computer science, mathematics or electives.
A course can count toward one group.\\
Transcript & The student's rows of course, credits, grade and term, imported from the portal PDF or typed in (UC-02).\\
Plan & An ordered list of terms from now to graduation, with the courses placed in each. A student may keep several plans.\\
Credit cap & The minimum and maximum number of credits allowed in one term by the program's regulation.\\
Rule engine & The deterministic part of OmniCourse that decides every academic rule: prerequisites, offering terms, credit
caps, requirement coverage, pass marks and graduation status (UC-19). It is ordinary tested code, never a language
model.\\
Violation & A rule the rule engine finds broken in a plan, with the rule and the regulation clause it comes from.\\
Goal box & The text box of UC-07 where a student writes a planning goal in their own words; a model turns it into the
fields of the planning-goal form.\\
Advisor chat & The F8 chat that answers questions from the regulations, with citations (UC-08).\\
Advisor & A lecturer or academic advisor who reads a student's shared plan (UC-16). Not the advisor chat.\\
Shared plan & A read-only view of one plan, opened by an unguessable link, with no account needed.\\
Guide & A versioned page that explains one school task done on a portal, with its direct link (UC-10).\\
\end{xltabular}}

\subsection{How to read a specification}
Every specification in section \ref{sec:specs} uses the course template: a header with the feature, actors, priority and
the build that delivers it; a brief description; preconditions; the basic flow; alternative flows; postconditions; special
requirements; and relationships to other use cases. Alternative flows are numbered A1, A2, and so on, and say at which step
of the basic flow they start and where they return. Words in \st{bold} are labels the user sees on screen. The three hard
rules of the project apply to every use case and are not repeated in each one:

\begin{ocnote}{Rules every use case follows}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A language model never decides an academic rule. Only the rule engine does (UC-19). A model is used in two places
        only, the goal box of UC-07 and the advisor chat of UC-08, and it may only explain the rule engine's results.
  \item OmniCourse never asks for a school-portal password and never registers, pays or acts on a portal for the student.
        It links to the portal.
  \item No personal data goes into prompts, logs or test data. Tests use synthetic students only.
\end{enumerate}
\end{ocnote}

uses. The module diagrams show the associations, the \guillemotleft include\guillemotright{} and

of that module has a heavy outline; one from another module is drawn light, so the reader can see where the module

\subsubsection{A. Account and access}
A visitor signs up or signs in, by email and password or with Google. Once signed in, the visitor is a student and manages
their own account: sessions, consent, export and deletion. Importing a transcript extends sign-up, because onboarding offers
it as its last, optional question.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{diagrams/uc-account.tex}\par\vspace{6pt}\uclegend
  \caption{Module A, account and access (F1).}
  \label{fig:account}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{B. Academic record}
The student brings in a transcript and reads where they stand: the degree audit per requirement group, the grades of each
term with what an unfinished course still needs, and the curriculum with each course's prerequisites. The degree audit,
the grades and the explorer all include UC-19, so the same rule engine decides requirement coverage, pass marks and which
courses are available or locked.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{diagrams/uc-record.tex}\par\vspace{6pt}\uclegend
  \caption{Module B, academic record (F2, F3, F4).}
  \label{fig:record}
\end{figure}

\clearpage
\subsubsection{C. Planning}
The student builds a plan by hand (UC-05) or has one generated from a planning goal (UC-07). A what-if scenario (UC-06)
extends the planner: it starts from an open plan and works on a copy. Every placement is checked by UC-19. The student can
share a plan (UC-09); the advisor opens the link and reads the plan, with the same violations the student sees (UC-16).
The model provider takes part only in UC-07, and only to turn a sentence into the fields of the planning-goal form.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{diagrams/uc-planning.tex}\par\vspace{6pt}\uclegend
  \caption{Module C, planning (F5, F6, F7, F9).}
  \label{fig:planning}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{D. Help and reminders}
Any visitor can read a guide and open the portal link it gives (UC-10). A signed-in student can ask the advisor chat
(UC-08), which uses the model provider to write the answer but asks UC-19 for anything about the student's own record.
Notifications (UC-12) are started by time, or by a change in the student's data, and use UC-19 to find what needs
attention.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \input{diagrams/uc-help.tex}\par\vspace{6pt}\uclegend
  \caption{Module D, help and reminders (F8, F10, F11).}
  \label{fig:help}
\end{figure}

\clearpage
\subsubsection{E. Curriculum administration}
Three separate permissions keep curriculum data safe. A program administrator edits one program's cohorts and requirement
groups (UC-11) and has the rule engine check what they enter. A change to a shared course record goes through the catalog
maintainer (UC-17), which extends UC-11 when the administrator needs a course that is not in the catalog yet. Only an access
manager creates programs and hands out these rights (UC-18).

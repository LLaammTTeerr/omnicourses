# ADR 0018: Notification inbox

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Deciders: Lâm (Project Lead)
- Owner of F10: Dũng
- Reviewers: Hoà (calendar and the inbox screens), Triết

## Context

F10 (Vision §5, UC-12) tells a student, inside the app, about a registration window, the start of a term and a change in their transcript. [ADR 0017](0017-academic-calendar.md) stores the school dates. Lâm decided on 12 November 2026 that B2 delivers through the in-app inbox only, with no email. The timing, the time zone and the repeat rules stayed open in the test plan §8.

**Dũng, the owner of F10, decided them on the team Discord on 25 November 2026** (message `1574971882556624929`). UC-12 does not require these values: its step 1 says only "for example seven days before a registration window opens". The seven rules below are Dũng's decision, in the short form in which it was passed on for this work; the Discord message holds his own words:

1. Registration window: one notification 7 days before it opens, and one on the day it opens.
2. Term start: one notification on the first day of the term.
3. Transcript change: one notification each time the student saves a transcript that differs from the saved one. None for the first import, for a cancelled or failed save, or for a save with no changes.
4. Days follow Vietnam time (UTC+7). A day's notification exists by 00:00 that day.
5. In the app only (inbox and bell), no email. (Lâm decided this on 12 November 2026.)
6. No repeats: each student gets each notification once. If a date is entered late, the notification that is due now is sent and the ones whose day has passed are skipped. If a date moves after a notification went out, nothing is sent again.
7. Not in B2: add/drop reminders, "plan breaks a rule" (UC-12 A2), turning a kind off (UC-12 A5).

**Dũng confirmed these points the same day** (message `1574974093835501679`):

- The recipients rule of this ADR (decision 6 below).
- A date entered for today gives its notification at once. A date entered exactly 7 days ahead gives its 7-day notification at once, the same way.
- The registration notification does not hold the plan's courses.
- The transcript notification holds no grade and no list of audit changes.
- A notification already sent stays when an administrator deletes the school date.

The rest of this ADR is the mechanism that carries the rules. The mechanism is the builder's proposal, not Dũng's or Lâm's decision. The status stays **proposed** until Lâm decides.

This ADR is 0018 because 0016 is claimed by the advisor harness (open PR #77).

## Decisions

The tests named in the Evidence column are in `src/backend/test/notifications.e2e-spec.ts` unless another file is named.

| Decision | Alternatives rejected | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. One function creates what is due, and two callers run it.** `NotificationsService.createDue()` creates every notification that is due on today's Vietnam day and does not exist yet. A timer provider (`NotificationSchedule`) calls it when the app starts, then every 10 minutes, and at 00:00 in Vietnam: each run books the next one at the sooner of the two. Each inbox read and each unread-count read calls it first, narrowed to the caller. The timer is a plain unref'd `setTimeout`; no dependency is added. | `@nestjs/schedule` or a cron library; an operating-system cron job that calls a route; the timer alone; the reads alone | The timer alone makes a tester wait up to 10 minutes after entering a date. The reads alone give nothing to a student who does not open the app that day, and that notification is lost when the day ends (decision 2). The account purge already runs on a plain timer for the same reason ([ADR 0004](0004-account-export-and-deletion.md)): one idempotent function needs no scheduler. Tests: "shows a date entered for today at once, and the seven-day notice of a date entered exactly seven days ahead at once"; "creates the day's notifications from the timer, without any read"; `notification-schedule.spec.ts`. |
| **2. "Due on day D" is strict, and a missed day is skipped.** The 7-day notification is created only on the day 7 days before `starts_on`. The opening-day and term-start notifications are created only on `starts_on`. Nothing remembers the last run and nothing catches up. | A catch-up window (send what was missed in the last N days); a stored "last run" day | Dũng's rule 6: skip the notifications whose day has passed. A catch-up window would announce "opens in 7 days" 5 days before the opening. **If the server is down for a whole Vietnam day, the notifications of that day are lost.** This follows rule 6: when the server is back, their day has passed. Tests: "skips the seven-day notice of a date entered three days before it starts, and still announces the opening day"; "does not catch up on a day that passed with no run"; `notification-kinds.spec.ts`. |
| **3. The day comes from an injected clock, in Vietnam time.** `createDue()` reads `Clock.now()` and `vietnamDate()` (UTC+7, no daylight saving). It never reads `CURRENT_DATE` or the database's time zone. `created_at` and `read_at` are written from the same clock. | The database's date; the server's local time zone | Dũng's rule 4. The database and the server run in UTC, where the day changes 7 hours late. An injected clock lets a test set the instant: 16:59:59.999 UTC is still the earlier Vietnam day and 17:00:00.000 UTC is the next one. Tests: "changes day at 00:00 in Vietnam, 17:00:00 UTC, exactly"; `config/time.spec.ts`. |
| **4. "By 00:00" is met by the timer and, for a reader, by the read.** The timer aims at 17:00:00 UTC, so the rows exist within moments of midnight. A student who reads the inbox or the bell at any time from 00:00 sees the day's notifications, because the read creates them first. | Create the rows the evening before with a "show from" time | A row created before its day would need a second rule to hide it, and a date changed in the evening would leave a wrong row. The read-time run gives the same result to the student with no extra state. |
| **5. No repeats: a uniqueness key on (student, school date, kind), never on the day.** `UNIQUE (user_id, calendar_event_id, kind)`, and the insert uses `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. Two runs at once, a second run the same day and a date that moves all insert nothing twice. A transcript notification has no school date (`calendar_event_id` is NULL), and PostgreSQL treats NULLs as distinct, so the key does not limit it. A CHECK keeps `calendar_event_id` NULL exactly for the kind `transcript_changed`, so no school-date notification can pass the key. | A key that holds the date (a moved date would send again); "select, then insert" (two runs at once would both insert); an advisory lock around the run | Dũng's rule 6. The key is per notification kind: when a date moves after the 7-day notification went out but before the opening day, the opening-day notification is still sent, on the new day, because it had not gone out. Tests: "sends nothing again when a date moves after its notification went out"; "makes one notification when several runs and reads happen at once"; "holds the no-repeat rule in the database: the key is the student, the date and the kind, never the day". |
| **6. Recipients: the students whose saved onboarding program is the date's program when the notification is made, without the accounts waiting for deletion.** One `INSERT … SELECT` per school date and kind writes the rows of all recipients; there is no loop per student. A student who joins the program later the same day still gets that day's notifications at their next read or at the next timer run. A student who joins the next day gets only later notifications. An account with `deletion_requested_at` set gets nothing; if the deletion is cancelled, it gets the notifications made from then on. The timer run and the read run use the same query; the read run adds the caller's ID. | Every student of every program; a row per student written in a loop; notifications also for accounts in the deletion grace period | Confirmed by Dũng on 25 November 2026. The calendar is per program (ADR 0017). An account waiting for deletion has no session and may be purged ([ADR 0004](0004-account-export-and-deletion.md)); new rows for it would only be deleted. A program counts as soon as onboarding has saved it, finished or not: this is the rule the calendar read uses for its default program, and it is the builder's choice (open question 1). Tests: "gives each student the notifications of their own program, never another program's"; "gives a student who joins the program only the notifications made from then on"; "makes nothing for an account waiting for deletion, and resumes when the deletion is cancelled"; "counts a program as soon as it is saved, before onboarding is finished". |
| **7. A deleted school date keeps its notifications: no foreign key, and a copy of the date in the row.** `calendar_event_id` is a plain `uuid` column. The row holds a copy of the program code, academic year, term, `starts_on`, `ends_on`, title and English title, taken when the notification is made. The copy is never updated. A date entered again after a deletion has a new ID, so it is a new date and makes its own notifications. | `ON DELETE CASCADE` (a notification the student already read would vanish); `ON DELETE SET NULL` (the notification would lose what it announced, and the NULL would leave the key); `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (an administrator could not delete a wrong date); a soft delete in the calendar | Confirmed by Dũng on 25 November 2026: notifications already sent stay. The calendar kind is not copied, because the notification kind implies it. The source link is not copied: decision 9 allows no URL. Because the copy is not updated, a notification shows the date as it was announced; the calendar page shows the current date, and every school-date notification links to it. Test: "keeps the notification already sent, and a date entered again is a new date with its own notifications". |
| **8. The transcript notification is written inside the transcript save's own transaction.** `TranscriptService.replace()` reads the stored rows under its user lock, replaces them, reads them again and compares the two sets as a multiset: the order does not matter, and a retake is a row of its own. It then calls its listeners with `{ userId, wasEmpty, changed }` and the transaction. The inbox registers a listener and inserts one row when `changed` is true and `wasEmpty` is false. "First import" means that nothing was stored before the save. So saving rows over an empty transcript makes no notification, also after the student emptied it; emptying a saved transcript is a change and makes one. A transcript notification has no uniqueness key: each differing save makes one. | Compare in the inbox module after the commit (a failed save could leave a notification, or a crash could lose one); compare the request with the stored rows (a grade sent as 7.34 is stored as 7.3); import the inbox module into the transcript module | Dũng's rule 3. A save that is refused never reaches the transaction. A save that fails inside it rolls the notification back with the rows. The other side of this choice: if the notification cannot be written, the save fails too. The listener keeps the transcript module free of the inbox, and it is told only "changed", never a row. Tests: the group "a changed transcript"; `test/transcript.e2e-spec.ts` ("tells a listener, inside the transaction, …", "rolls the save back when a listener fails"); `transcript-change.spec.ts`. |
| **9. A notification is a kind and data, never text.** Kinds: `registration_soon`, `registration_open`, `term_start`, `transcript_changed`. The API returns the kind, the copy of the school date (or null), the times, and link targets as stable keys: `calendar`, `planner`, `audit`. The screen words the notification in Vietnamese and English and maps a key to its own route. No URL, no sentence, no grade, no course code and no rule value is stored or returned. | Store the Vietnamese sentence; store a URL per notification; send the calendar's `source_url` | The app has two languages, and a stored sentence could not follow a wording fix. A typed URL is an injection path (ADR 0017 limits `source_url` for the same reason). UC-12 says "a notification never states a rule's value on its own"; this one states none (AGENTS.md hard rules 1 and 3). Tests: "returns only keys the screen words itself: no sentence, no URL, no user ID"; "says only that the transcript changed: no grade, no course, no audit result". |
| **10. Two parts of UC-12 are not built in B2 and are B3 work.** UC-12 steps 2–3 put "your plan places N courses, M credits" in the registration notification. UC-12 A1 says the transcript notification "lists what changed in the audit". Neither is built: the registration notification holds the date only, and the transcript notification says only that the transcript changed and links to the degree audit. | Count the plan's courses in the inbox module; compare two audits in the inbox module | Confirmed by Dũng on 25 November 2026. Both values would have to come from the rule engine (and the plans module), never from this module (AGENTS.md hard rule 1). They are recorded in [design-differences.md](../design-differences.md) as B3 work. |
| **11. Four routes, all behind sign-in.** `GET /api/notifications`: the caller's notifications, newest first (`created_at`, then `id`, both descending), with the filters `unread` and `kind` (repeatable), `limit` (default 50, at most 100) and `before` (the ID of the last notification of the previous page). `GET /api/notifications/unread-count`: the number for the bell. `POST /api/notifications/{id}/read` and `POST /api/notifications/read-all` (UC-12 A3): both can be repeated without effect, and `readAt` keeps the first time. No route takes a user ID. Another student's ID, an unknown ID and a malformed ID all answer `404 not_found`, and nothing changes. | A 403 for another student's notification; a 400 for a malformed ID; a page number; a timestamp as the page cursor | A 403 or a 400 would tell an ID that exists from one that does not; the plans routes answer 404 for the same reason ([ADR 0014](0014-plan-model.md)). The page cursor is resolved inside PostgreSQL, because a timestamp read into JavaScript loses its microseconds and a page could skip or repeat a row. No route is public, so the allow-list of `public-routes.e2e-spec.ts` does not change ([ADR 0002](0002-authentication-and-sessions.md)). Tests: the group "the inbox routes"; "refuses every route without a token". |
| **12. Export and purge need no extra code.** The table has its own `user_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES users(id) ON DELETE CASCADE`. No column name ends in a credential suffix. | A registration step for the export | [ADR 0004](0004-account-export-and-deletion.md): the export reads every table with a `user_id` key to `users`, and the purge cascades. Tests: "exports the student's notifications, and removes them with the account"; the catalog checks of `account.e2e-spec.ts` pass unchanged. |
| **13. The module reads other modules through three small exported methods.** `AcademicCalendarService.startingOn(days, kinds)` (read-only): the dates of the given kinds that start on the given days. `OnboardingService.userIdsByProgram(codes, onlyUserId?)` (read-only): the students of the given programs. `TranscriptService.onSaved(listener)`: the hook of decision 8. No module imports the notifications module. | One SQL statement that joins `academic_calendar`, `programs` and `onboarding_answers` from the notifications module; a call from the transcript module into the inbox | The first alternative is one statement fewer, but it reads three tables that other modules own. The price of the boundary: the recipient IDs of one program pass through the server's memory, then one `INSERT … SELECT` writes all rows. The statement reads `users.deletion_requested_at` directly; `users` is the table every module references (AGENTS.md), and F12's write lock reads the same column the same way. |
| **14. Not built in B2.** Add/drop reminders (an `add_drop` date makes no notification), "plan breaks a rule" (UC-12 A2), and turning a kind off (UC-12 A5). No model is used anywhere in this module. | | Dũng's rule 7. Test: "makes nothing for add/drop windows and the other kinds of school date". |

## Consequences

- Hoà's inbox and bell read four routes. The screens are not started. The bell can poll `unread-count`; on a day with no date due, each poll adds one small query to the count.
- A notification of a date entered during a day reaches a student who does not open the app within 10 minutes. A date entered in the last minutes of its day can miss the students who do not read before midnight: when the next run comes, the day has passed (rule 6).
- If the server is down for a whole Vietnam day, that day's notifications are lost (decision 2). Nothing reports the gap.
- A notification shows the school date as it was announced. If the date moves, nothing is sent again (rule 6) and the old notification keeps the old date; the calendar page shows the current one.
- The uniqueness key is per student. If a date moves, a student who joined the program after the first notification went out can still get that notification on the new day, because they never had it.
- Notifications are never deleted, except with the account. There is no retention rule yet; a student who saves a different transcript many times gets one notification per save.
- The app runs as one instance. Two instances would both run the timer; the uniqueness key makes that harmless.
- A new notification kind needs a migration (the CHECK) and an amendment here.

## Open questions

1. For Dũng: does a student count as a recipient as soon as onboarding has saved a program (as built), or only after onboarding is finished (the precondition of UC-12)?
2. For Dũng: when a date moves after the 7-day notification went out, the opening-day notification is still sent on the new opening day (decision 5). Is this the wanted reading of rule 6?
3. For Dũng and Hoà: the link keys per kind are `calendar` and `planner` for a registration notification, `calendar` for a term start, and `audit` for a transcript change. UC-12 step 3 also links the registration guide (UC-10); no stable key for a guide exists yet.
4. For Lâm: the timer interval (10 minutes) and the absence of a retention rule.
